# Kinetic Foundry — Game Design Authority v0.1

## North star
A grounded 3D industrial-dystopia action game where **power, combat, and
machines are one continuous physical sandbox**. Spectacle must come from
causality: force, mass, leverage, damage, structure, and reusable physical
consequences.

## Player
The player is an enhanced industrial brute: recognizably human but strong,
resilient, and athletic enough to overpower people, rip light metal, climb
moving machinery, survive hard impacts, and physically interfere with the
industrial world. Heavy machinery must still dwarf and outclass the player.

## Movement and camera
- Athletic grounded traversal: sprint, vault, mantle, climb machinery, jump
  realistic gaps, slide, scramble across debris, traverse moving equipment.
- Pulled-back third-person camera for machine scale, battlefield awareness,
  destruction readability, and Fold 6 usability.
- No superhero traversal and no camera theatrics as a substitute for force.

## Combat and interaction
- Hybrid authored + physics combat.
- Character-relative interaction; the player expresses intent and the game
  resolves the reachable target.
- Adaptive lock: soft targeting by default, stronger temporary commitment
  during clinches, grapples, and close exchanges.
- Core verbs: punch, kick, tackle, clinch, grab, shove, throw, slam,
  environmental impact, improvised weapon, machine-assisted violence.

## Machine law
A major machine counts only if it exposes several qualitatively different,
consequential verbs and visibly changes the world. Tiny gears, pulleys,
clutches, valves, belts, pistons, and similar internals may support the model,
but they are never accepted as flagship gameplay when the player can only
watch, toggle, or rotate them.

Machine controls are hybrid:
- simple locomotion;
- direct control of the primary working assembly;
- compact contextual actions for discrete physical intent.

## First flagship machine: excavator
The excavator must support: drive, steer, articulate boom/stick/tool, grab,
lift, drag, shove, brace, smash, pull, tear, demolish, manipulate debris,
attack enemies, alter structures, create routes, sustain damage, and remain
physically relevant after abandonment or failure.

## Destruction
Structural destruction is the default. Strategically important objects receive
deeper material response. Supports, load paths, asymmetric loading, impacts,
bending, buckling, fracture, shifting mass, and loss of bracing should create
persistent changes to navigation and combat.

## Enemy ecosystem
Mostly human factions use the same industrial world as the player. They may
commandeer machines, reposition barriers, destroy cover, reclaim equipment,
abandon damaged machinery, fight rival groups, and manipulate environmental
hazards.

## Campaign
A hybrid industrial campaign with distinct physical objectives and long-term
control of increasingly important facilities and infrastructure. Missions are
physical problems, not repeated kill rooms.

## Progression
Character upgrades + equipment upgrades. Growth should create materially new
or stronger capability, not tiny statistical sludge.

## World identity
Grounded industrial dystopia: factories, foundries, shipyards, mines,
quarries, freight yards, refineries, steelworks, demolition sites,
construction zones, storage complexes, and machine depots. Escalation remains
mechanically legible and never drifts into abstract technological magic.

## Fold 6 control law
Landscape, touch-first, low precision burden. Left thumb locomotion, right
thumb camera or machine assembly, compact contextual actions, forgiving touch
regions, adaptive targeting, and protected gameplay visibility.

## Coupling law
Combat affects machines. Machines affect structures. Structures affect
traversal. Damage affects machine capability. Machine failure creates debris.
Debris becomes cover, weapon, obstacle, load, brace, or traversal geometry.
Enemy AI and progression operate through these same authorities.

## First vertical slice
1. Player moves through an industrial yard on foot.
2. Player fights human enemies.
3. Character-relative grab and adaptive targeting work.
4. An enemy contests or operates an excavator.
5. Player commandeers the excavator.
6. Locomotion and direct arm control work.
7. Excavator affects enemies, debris, and structure.
8. At least one structure fails progressively.
9. Failure changes navigation or combat.
10. Player can dismount.
11. Wreckage remains physically relevant.

The slice succeeds only when on-foot power, combat, machine operation, and
structural consequence feel like one game.
